# The Carpathian Brown Bear Monitoring Network: Methods and Findings

## 1. Introduction

The Carpathian Brown Bear Monitoring Network (CBBMN) is a long-term research
program studying the brown bear (Ursus arctos) population of the Ukrainian
Western Carpathians. It was founded in 2014 by the Carpathian Wildlife
Institute in Uzhhorod, following two decades of fragmented, single-institute
surveys whose results could not be compared across years or regions. The
network's goal is to produce a continuous, methodologically consistent series
of population estimates across the whole Ukrainian Carpathian range, and to
publish them in both English and Ukrainian so that researchers, conservation
officers, and local communities work from the same numbers. The program
coordinates with equivalent efforts in Poland and Slovakia through data
exchange rather than joint fieldwork, so the Ukrainian estimates can be
compared against the wider Carpathian meta-population. A small advisory
board of wildlife biologists and park managers reviews each annual cycle
before publication.

## 2. Study area and methods

The network operates in three regions of the Ukrainian Western Carpathians:
Hutsulshchyna, the Syvuly mountains, and the Western Beskids, together
covering the core of the contiguous forested area where bears are regularly
recorded. Data collection relies on two complementary methods. The first is a
grid of 128 camera traps deployed along forest trails and game paths; each
camera is checked monthly, and photographs are reviewed independently by two
readers before an individual is confirmed. The second method is satellite
telemetry: GPS collars have been fitted on 23 individual bears, allowing the
network to track movements, seasonal range shifts, and home-range size with
metre-level accuracy. Camera sites are chosen to sit at natural funnel points where bears move
between feed sites, and the grid is rotated each year so that no single
region dominates the sample. Bears are identified from camera photographs
by pelage and facial pattern, and collar captures cross-validate those
identifications, so the two methods reinforce each other.

## 3. Key findings

The 2023 population estimate for the Ukrainian Western Carpathians is 1,800
to 2,200 individuals, up from the 2014 baseline of 1,600. The network
attributes the increase to a sustained reduction in poaching pressure and to
a stable food base across the study regions. Average home-range size was 180
square kilometres for females and 410 square kilometres for males, and
youngest-recruit sightings appeared in all three regions during the 2022 and
2023 seasons, which the network reads as evidence of a recovering,
dispersing population rather than a closed one. Each annual cycle publishes an
open dataset of confirmed individual detections, a range map, and a
population estimate with uncertainty bounds, all released under a shared
license for research reuse.

## 4. Governance and funding

The network is coordinated by Dr. Irena Koval and is funded by the
Alpine-Transcarpathian Conservation Trust. Annual reports are published in
English and Ukrainian. Raw camera-trap and telemetry records are archived
with a five-year lag to protect the exact locations of sensitive individuals.
